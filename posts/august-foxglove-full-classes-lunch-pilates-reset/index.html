August at Foxglove: Full Classes, 30-Minute Lunches, and the Final Days of the Reset

2026-09-22

A Busy Month in the West End

We shut the doors on Monday, August 3 for our annual deep clean and staff training day, and it turned out to be the quietest the studio would be all month. Everything after that was full. If you've been doing reformer Pilates in Toronto long enough, you know August is usually the sleepy one. Not here, not this year.

Marco's Breath & Core workshop on August 15 took all 12 spots. We had people asking about a second date before the first one had even happened, which is a nice problem and one we're thinking about for the fall.

The pattern we keep noticing at our Pilates studio in the west end of Toronto is that the classes filling fastest aren't the long ones or the fancy ones. They're the ones that fit into a gap in somebody's day. Thirty minutes at lunch. Forty-five on a Saturday morning before the rest of the weekend starts. That tells us something, and we're building the September schedule around it.

Making Time at Lunch

The Tuesday 12:15pm Lunch Express Reformer class runs 30 minutes. Not 45, not 50. Thirty.

That number wasn't arbitrary. We mapped it against how far somebody can actually walk from an office or a home desk in the neighbourhood, change, do the work, and get back without eating into the afternoon. Once you account for the walk both ways, 30 minutes on the reformer is what fits. Longer and the whole thing stops being realistic on a Tuesday.

People sometimes assume a shorter lunchtime Pilates class means a watered-down one. It doesn't. You skip the long settling-in and you get on the machine. Springs, footwork, the standing series, done. Most people leave looser than they arrived and still have time to eat something at their desk.

We're at 118 Wrenfield Ave, Unit 3, which is the other half of why this works. If you're in the west end, you're probably already within walking distance. That's the whole design.

Growing the Prenatal Schedule

Priya's 45-minute Prenatal Mat class runs Saturdays at 9:00am, and it is now the busiest Saturday class we run. Not the busiest prenatal class. The busiest class, full stop.

We didn't expect that when we put it on the schedule. Prenatal Pilates in Toronto tends to get treated as a niche add-on, tucked into an odd hour when the room is free. Turns out if you give it a real time slot with a teacher who knows what she's doing, people show up.

So we're adding a second prenatal class to the September schedule. Details will go up on the booking page as soon as the timetable is locked. If you've been on the Saturday waitlist, that second class is largely for you.

A note for anyone who's been hesitating: this class draws a lot of first-timers. Being new to Pilates and pregnant at the same time is a completely normal way to walk in the door. Priya works with where you are.

Last Call for the August Reset

The August Reset ends on August 31 at 11:59pm. After that the code stops working, and we don't do exceptions on this one because the reason it's called August Reset is that it's in August.

Here's the plain math on our Pilates class packs:

  • A 10-class pack is normally $290.
  • Code RESET20 takes 20 percent off, bringing it to $232.
  • The pack expires six months from purchase.
  • It can be transferred to one named person.

That transfer rule is worth knowing about. If you buy ten and life gets in the way, you can hand the remainder to one person you name — a partner, a friend, whoever. It's not a general gift card, but it means the pack isn't stranded if your schedule turns sideways in October.

Six months is a reasonable runway for most people. If you come roughly twice a month you'll finish it comfortably. If you're aiming for weekly, you'll be back for another pack before the expiry is anywhere close.

Getting Started

If you've never been to Foxglove, don't start with a ten-pack. Start with the intro offer: three classes for $59, valid for 30 days. It works on any class on the schedule except the members-only Sunday Open Studio. Three classes is about the right number to figure out whether the reformer makes sense for your body and whether the room feels like somewhere you want to keep coming back to.

If group classes aren't where you want to begin — and for some people coming back from injury, they aren't — private reformer sessions with Dana or Marco are $95 for 60 minutes, or five sessions for $440. A couple of privates first, then into a class, is a perfectly good route and one we recommend often.

One practical thing: grip socks are required on the reformers. If you don't own a pair, we sell them at the desk for $18. Nobody's ever been turned away for forgetting, but it's easier if you know before you arrive.

So: buy the 10-class pack with code RESET20 before August 31, or grab the 3-class intro offer if you're new to the studio. Both are on the booking page.
